3.2 Salt-dependent phase behaviour of positively charged globular proteins At low salt concentrations (typically < 100 mM for monovalent salts), direct anion binding to positively charged protein surfaces dominates the interaction landscape. This binding follows the inverse Hofmeister series, where larger, more polarizable anions (SCN⁻ > I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻) exhibit stronger binding affinity and consequently lower the cloud-point temperature (Tcloud) more effectively. The thermodynamic basis for this behavior can be understood through the free energy of anion binding: ΔGbind = ΔHelectrostatic + ΔHhydration − TΔSconfig Experimental measurements using isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) confirm that the enthalpic contribution from electrostatic interactions dominates at low salt, while entropic contributions from water release play a secondary role. At high salt concentrations (> 200 mM), the electrostatic contribution becomes screened. Under these conditions, ion polarizability and the surface tension at the protein-water interface emerge as the dominant factors. This transition marks the crossover from inverse to direct Hofmeister behavior, where Cl⁻ now stabilizes the folded/phase-separated state more effectively than SCN⁻. Table 3 summarizes the experimental phase-transition temperatures for lysozyme across four representative sodium salts (50-200 mM), confirming the predicted crossover at approximately 150 mM NaCl.
